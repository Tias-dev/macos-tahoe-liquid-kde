  to an unrelated or unmodified upstream tree is not source for your changes.

  commercial redistribution, or renaming (section 10).

These are a summary, not additional license terms. Consult the full license
for its definitions, exceptions, and distribution methods. Independent works

licenses; inclusion here does not automatically convert every file to GPLv3.
Private modifications do not by themselves require public source publication.

The project currently charges no fee and does not solicit donations. That is
a project decision, not a noncommercial license restriction. GPL-compliant

## Attribution and independent versions

Keep required legal notices intact, including those of upstream authors.
Adding your own copyright notice does not replace notices for others' work.
The project asks downstream README files to identify the source project, link
to the relevant repository, and explain the changes and independent maintainer.
This README presentation is a request, not a new GPL condition; the GPL's
requirements for legal and modification notices still apply.

An attribution example, to adapt to the actual source and changes:

> Independent adaptation of macos-tahoe-liquid-kde, maintained by [name].
> Original project: https://github.com/lestercorderomurillo/macos-tahoe-liquid-kde.
> Changes: [description and date]. This version is not an official project release.

Use Golden Gate's repository when that is the source of your adaptation.
See [project identity, forks, and evidence](#project-identity-forks-and-evidence)
for official channels, accurate release claims,
and handling suspected impersonation or missing notices.

### Represent your work accurately

Forks can choose their own names and add their own authorship notices. They
must still preserve notices required by the applicable licenses and mark
GPL-covered modifications with a relevant date. We ask independent projects to:

- Identify their maintainer and source project, and make their independent
  status clear where users download or read about the software.
- Describe their actual changes, including when those changes are limited
  to names, packaging, or screenshots. Do not claim others' work as wholly
  original or describe an independent release as maintained or endorsed here.
- Use their own support and download destinations rather than suggesting
  that the original maintainers support the downstream build.
- Label reused screenshots as illustrations from the original project,
  identifying the source and any edits. Do not present them as captures of a
  different build or as proof of testing that has not occurred.

The presentation requests above do not impose an extra GPL attribution banner
or an approval process for forks. False affiliation and impersonation may
separately violate applicable law or the hosting platform's policies.

### Screenshots and validation claims

Published captures, reports, and their hashes describe the recorded edition,
source revision, and test environment. They do not certify a modified fork.
A hash checks integrity against a trusted reference; it does not independently
prove who produced an image or that a different build behaves the same way.

When discussing upstream results, link to the original report and state what
was tested. Claims about a downstream build should be backed by that build's
own evidence. Do not relabel upstream reports as downstream test runs.
Screenshot reuse remains subject to applicable rights in the image and its
visible third-party material; this guidance grants no rights in that material.

### Report a concrete concern

Send a factual report through the relevant canonical repository's Issues page.
Include the original file or release URL and commit, the downstream URL and
commit if available, the specific removed notice or misleading claim, and a
comparison showing the issue. Explain which work and rights are involved.
Do not include private information or encourage harassment.

Maintainers should review ownership, applicable licenses, permitted uses, and
the evidence before seeking a correction. A fork, rebrand, commercial sale,
or lack of new features alone is not a GPL violation. A public README credit
request alone is not grounds for a copyright takedown.

Use the appropriate platform process for a substantiated concern:

- [GitHub impersonation policy](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-impersonation).
- [GitHub trademark policy](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/github-trademark-policy); its evidence and registration requirements must actually be met.
- [GitHub copyright notice guide](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/guide-to-submitting-a-dmca-takedown-notice); consider licensed use and fair use, establish authority over the specific work, and obtain legal review before filing.

Do not file automated or blanket complaints against forks. Copyright,
trademark, and impersonation complaints are different processes; a claim under
one does not establish a claim under another.
